Two mirrors, one angle θ — four famous conditions.

Derive the deviation formula, then solve the four classic "find the condition" problems that appear in every JEE paper. Drag the ray, drag the mirror, watch the math come alive.

Net deviation after two reflections

Setup

Two plane mirrors $M_1$ and $M_2$ meet at a vertex $V$ with interior angle $\theta$. A ray enters the wedge, reflects once off each mirror, and exits. We want the total angular deviation $\delta$ between the incident and emergent directions.

Result $$\delta = 2\pi - 2\theta$$

Derivation (scroll-scrubbed)

Let the ray strike $M_1$ at glancing angle $\alpha_1$ and $M_2$ at glancing angle $\alpha_2$. Inside the triangle formed by the two hit points and the vertex, the angles sum to $\pi$:

$(90^\circ - \alpha_1) + (90^\circ - \alpha_2) + \theta = 180^\circ \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = \theta.$

Each reflection deviates the ray by twice the glancing angle. Total deviation:

$\delta = 2\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 = 2(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2) = 2\theta.$

Measured as the exterior turning angle (the standard convention for a ray that completes a full U-turn), this is $\delta = 2\pi - 2\theta$. Crucially, the result is independent of the angle of incidence — it depends only on the wedge angle $\theta$.

Common trap

$\delta = 2\pi - 2\theta$ applies only to a ray that hits both mirrors. If the ray escapes after one reflection, the deviation is $\delta = \pi - 2i$ instead — see Q4.

Q1 · Emergent ray parallel to incident

Q1 For what $\theta$ is the emergent ray parallel to the incident ray?

Two plane mirrors are inclined at angle $\theta$. A ray reflects off both. Find $\theta$ such that the final ray is parallel to the incident ray.

Reveal step-by-step solution
Step 1. "Parallel" here means the emergent ray travels along the same line as the incident ray, but in the opposite direction — i.e. it is anti-parallel. The slide's wording "parallel" is loose; the precise statement is retro-reflection.
Step 2. Anti-parallel means the deviation is exactly $\delta = \pi$ (a 180° U-turn).
Step 3. Use $\delta = 2\pi - 2\theta = \pi \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; 2\theta = \pi \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 90^\circ$.
✓ Answer: $\theta = 90^\circ$ — the corner-reflector (retro-reflector) property.

Q2 · Ray parallel to one mirror in, parallel to the other out

Q2 Ray arrives parallel to $M_1$ and leaves parallel to $M_2$. Find $\theta$.

The incident ray travels parallel to $M_1$. After two reflections, the emergent ray travels parallel to $M_2$. Find the wedge angle $\theta$.

Reveal step-by-step solution
Step 1. Place $M_1$ along the $x$-axis and $M_2$ at angle $\theta$. The incident ray is parallel to $M_1$ and travelling toward the vertex, so its direction is $\mathbf{d}_1 = (-\cos\theta,\; -\sin\theta)$ (parallel to $M_2$ would be the symmetric case).
Step 2. Reflect in $M_1$ (normal $\mathbf{n}_1 = (0,1)$): $\mathbf{d}_2 = \mathbf{d}_1 - 2(\mathbf{d}_1\cdot\mathbf{n}_1)\mathbf{n}_1 = (-\cos\theta,\; +\sin\theta)$.
Step 3. Reflect in $M_2$ with inward normal $\mathbf{n}_2 = (\sin\theta,\; -\cos\theta)$. The $y$-component of $\mathbf{d}_3$ must vanish for the ray to be parallel to $M_1$ — wait, we want it parallel to $M_2$, so the component perpendicular to $M_2$ must vanish. That gives $\sin\theta - 2\sin 2\theta\cos\theta = 0$.
Step 4. Simplify: $\sin\theta(1 - 4\cos^2\theta) = 0$. Since $\theta \neq 0$, we need $\cos^2\theta = \tfrac{1}{4}$, so $\cos\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\theta = 60^\circ$.
✓ Answer: $\theta = 60^\circ$.

Q3 · Light retraces its path

Q3 For what incidence angle does the ray retrace its path after the second reflection?

A ray strikes $M_1$ at angle of incidence $i_1$, reflects to $M_2$, and then retraces its original path exactly. Find $i_1$ in terms of $\theta$.

Reveal step-by-step solution
Step 1. For the ray to retrace its path, it must return along the same line it came from. After reflecting off $M_2$, the ray must head straight back to the $M_1$ hit-point along the first segment.
Step 2. This happens if and only if the ray strikes $M_2$ normally — i.e. at $90^\circ$ to the mirror surface, so the angle of incidence on $M_2$ is zero.
Step 3. Inside the triangle formed by the vertex and the two hit points, the angles at $M_1$ and $M_2$ are the glancing angles $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ - i_1$ and $\alpha_2 = 0$. Since $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \theta = 180^\circ$ is wrong — the correct triangle sum gives $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = \theta$, so $\alpha_1 = \theta$.
Step 4. Therefore $i_1 = 90^\circ - \alpha_1 = 90^\circ - \theta$… wait — re-checking: the angle of incidence is measured from the normal, so $i_1 = 90^\circ - \alpha_1$. With $\alpha_1 = \theta$, we get $i_1 = 90^\circ - \theta$. But the standard statement is $i_1 = \theta$ when the glancing angle is defined as $\alpha = 90^\circ - i$. Let me restate cleanly: the glancing angle at $M_1$ equals $\theta$, so $i_1 = 90^\circ - \theta$. However, many textbooks state the result as $i_1 = \theta$ using a different convention. The invariant is: the ray must strike $M_2$ normally.
✓ Answer: glancing angle $\alpha_1 = \theta$, equivalently $i_1 = 90^\circ - \theta$ (ray strikes $M_2$ at normal incidence).

Q4 · Ray never reaches the second mirror

Q4 Under what conditions does the ray miss $M_2$ entirely?

A ray strikes $M_1$. For what values of $\theta$ and $i_1$ does the reflected ray never hit $M_2$?

Reveal step-by-step solution
Case A — $\theta \geq 90^\circ$. The wedge is so open that any ray reflecting off $M_1$ (away from the vertex) will escape without ever crossing $M_2$. This holds for every angle of incidence.
Case B — $\theta < 90^\circ$. Let $\alpha = 90^\circ - i_1$ be the glancing angle at $M_1$. If the ray is travelling away from the vertex after reflection, it misses $M_2$ if and only if $\alpha \leq \theta$, i.e. $i_1 \geq 90^\circ - \theta$. If the ray is travelling toward the vertex, it always reaches $M_2$.
✓ Answer: $\theta \geq 90^\circ$ (any $i_1$), or $\theta < 90^\circ$ with $i_1 \geq 90^\circ - \theta$ and ray directed away from vertex.

The unfolding trick

Instead of reflecting the ray, reflect the wedge. Each copy is rotated by $\theta$ from the previous, and the ray becomes a single straight line. This is the single best intuition for Q4 — and it feeds directly into Module 11 (kaleidoscopes).

Stretch · Count of reflections

For a wedge of angle $\theta$ and a ray entering with glancing angle $\alpha$ at $M_1$, the total number of reflections before escape is

$$N = \left\lceil \frac{180^\circ - \alpha}{\theta} \right\rceil$$

This is exactly the number of wedge copies the straight ray crosses in the unfolding picture — try it in the tracer above.
