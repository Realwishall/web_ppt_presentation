Time in a Mirror

A vertical plane mirror beside a clock reverses the apparent time. Learn the one-line rule, then watch it live on an analog clock.

Actual · 5:00
Mirror · 7:00

The master rule

A vertical mirror swaps left and right. On a 12-hour dial, that is equivalent to a reflection about the 12–6 line, which is the same as subtracting the time from 12:00:00. To keep the arithmetic clean (especially when seconds are non-zero) we write it as a column subtraction from $11\!:\!59\!:\!60$:

$$T_{\text{mirror}} \;=\; 11\!:\!59\!:\!60 \;-\; T_{\text{actual}}$$

Worked digit-by-digit for $5\!:\!53\!:\!00 \to 6\!:\!07\!:\!00$:

borrow11 · 59 · 60
minus− 5 · 53 · 00
equals6 · 07 · 00

Shortcut. If minutes and seconds are both zero, just do $12 - h$. So $5\!:\!00\!:\!00 \to 7\!:\!00\!:\!00$ in one step.

Common trap. If seconds $\neq 0$ you must borrow twice — once from minutes, once from hours. Students who do $12\!:\!00\!:\!00 - T$ without borrowing get case (iii) below wrong. Always rewrite as $11\!:\!59\!:\!60 - T$.

Extension · Horizontal mirror

What if the mirror is placed horizontally above the clock instead? Now top and bottom swap — a reflection about the 3–9 line.

Horizontal-mirror rule

$$T_{\text{h-mirror}} \;=\; 6\!:\!00\!:\!00 \;-\; T_{\text{actual}} \pmod{12}$$

Example: $2\!:\!00\!:\!00$ above a horizontal mirror reads $4\!:\!00\!:\!00$. $8\!:\!30\!:\!00$ reads $5\!:\!30\!:\!00$ (since $6 - 8\!:\!30 \equiv 9\!:\!30$ mod 12 → actually $5\!:\!30$ when interpreted as the 12-hour reading — verify on your twin-clock by flipping it top-to-bottom).

The vertical-mirror rule uses the 12–6 axis; the horizontal-mirror rule uses the 3–9 axis. A mirror at any angle $\alpha$ through the centre reflects the dial by $2\alpha$.
