05 — DOWNFALL

Why Corpuscular Theory Collapsed

The theory was ultimately replaced by Wave Theory (Huygens, Young, Fresnel) due to phenomena that particles simply cannot explain.

✅ Rectilinear Propagation

Explained

Corpuscles travel in straight lines, producing sharp shadows (umbra and penumbra) from opaque obstacles — perfectly consistent.

✅ Reflection & Snell's Law

Explained

Elastic rebound gives ∠i = ∠r. An attractive normal force at the interface produces bending, matching Snell's law geometrically.

❌ Interference & Diffraction

Failed

Young's double-slit experiment produces fringes. Particles cannot cancel each other to create dark bands — waves can, via destructive interference.

❌ Polarization

Failed

Only transverse waves can be polarized. A scalar particle has no orientation, so the theory cannot explain polarizing crystals like tourmaline.

❌ Partial Reflection & Refraction

Failed

At a glass surface, some light reflects and some refracts simultaneously. Newton could not explain why identical corpuscles behave differently.

❌ Speed of Light in Media

Failed

Foucault's 1850 experiment proved vwater < vair, the opposite of Newton's prediction. The theory collapsed on its own terms.

Newton's Corpuscular Theory of Light

// Proposed 1675 · Published in "Opticks" 1704 · Replaced by Wave Theory ~1800

1. Definition

Sir Isaac Newton proposed that light consists of tiny, weightless, perfectly elastic particles called corpuscles, emitted at enormous speeds from luminous bodies and traveling in straight lines through empty space.

2. The Four Golden Postulates

  1. Particle nature: Light is composed of infinitesimally small, massless (or nearly massless), perfectly elastic corpuscles.
  2. Rectilinear motion: Corpuscles travel in perfectly straight lines due to their enormous velocity and inertia.
  3. Color = size: Different colors of light correspond to corpuscles of different sizes and masses (red = largest, violet = smallest).
  4. Vacuum propagation: Corpuscles travel through vacuum and empty space without requiring any medium — unlike sound.

3. Successes of the Theory

  • Sharp shadows: Explains the umbra and penumbra formed by opaque objects (rectilinear propagation).
  • Law of reflection: Elastic rebound off a smooth surface gives ∠i = ∠r, just like a bouncing ball.
  • Snell's law of refraction: An attractive perpendicular force at the interface bends the path toward the normal — geometrically consistent with n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.

4. The Fatal Error — Speed of Light

Newton's derivation of Snell's law required the corpuscle to accelerate when entering a denser medium. Therefore he predicted:

Newton: vdenser > vrarer Foucault (1850): vdenser < vrarer

Foucault's rotating-mirror experiment measured vwater ≈ 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s, confirming the wave theory and falsifying the corpuscular prediction.

5. Why the Theory Failed — Key Exam Points

  • Interference: Young's double-slit experiment shows fringes — corpuscles cannot interfere destructively to produce dark bands.
  • Diffraction: Light bends around obstacles and through narrow slits — particles travel in straight lines.
  • Polarization: Only transverse waves can be polarized. Corpuscles have no transverse character.
  • Simultaneous reflection & refraction: At a glass surface, some light reflects and some refracts at the same time. The theory cannot explain why identical corpuscles make different choices.
  • Speed measurement: Foucault (1850) proved vwater < vair, directly contradicting Newton's prediction.

6. Historical Verdict

Despite being elegant and geometrically consistent with many optical phenomena, the Corpuscular Theory was superseded by Huygens' Wave Theory, later refined by Fresnel and Maxwell, and ultimately by Einstein's photon (quantum theory, 1905) which reconciled both wave and particle behaviors through wave-particle duality.
