01 · Geometry

Two homes for one lens.

Object and screen sit a fixed distance D apart. Somewhere between them a convex lens focuses — and, remarkably, it focuses at a second station too. The two stations are x apart, and they simply swap u and v.

u₁ = (D−x)/2 v₁ = (D+x)/2 x D L₁ · magnified L₂ · diminished
Reversibility does the work Light paths run both ways. If (u, v) images O onto the screen, then a lens placed so that object-distance = old v and image-distance = old u images it just as well. Hence u and v swap between L₁ and L₂.
Magnitudes, real-is-positive All u, v, x, D here are positive lengths. From v − u = x and u + v = D:
Who is where L₁ sits near the object → u small, v big → magnified image (m₁ > 1). L₂ sits near the screen → diminished image (m₂ < 1). Both perfectly sharp.

02 · Derivation

Four lines to the working formula.

Scroll — the bench on the left redraws itself as each line of algebra lands.

STEP 1 / 4 — THE TWO STATIONS
STEP 1 · GEOMETRY

Two stations, one swap.

With O and the screen locked D apart, the lens sharpens at L₁ and again at L₂, separated by x. By reversibility, the conjugate distances simply exchange: subtract v − u = x from u + v = D and both positions fall out.

STEP 2 · THE LENS LAW

Apply it at either station.

Real-is-positive convention: at a sharp position 1/f = 1/v + 1/u, i.e. f = uv/(u+v). It doesn't matter which station you pick — the product and the sum are identical for both.

STEP 3 · SUBSTITUTE

D and x do all the talking.

The sum u+v is D by construction; the product uv = (D−x)(D+x)/4 by difference of squares. Two measurable bench lengths — no u, no v left in the answer.

STEP 4 · THE LIMIT

Squeeze x to zero.

As the two stations slide together, x → 0 and f → D/4. That is the limiting case: the two positions coincide at the midpoint, and D = 4f is the smallest separation that forms a real image at all. Below it, the bench goes dark.

Trap · the classic slip x is the distance between the two lens positions — not between object and lens. Mixing these up is the single most common error in this derivation (and in JEE's version of it).

f versus x · the operating curve

For the current D, every possible lens-gap x implies a focal length f = (D²−x²)/4D. The curve peaks at f = D/4 when x = 0 — the merged-position limit. Your snapped measurement drops onto the curve as the amber point.

How to run the real experiment

  • Rough f first: focus a distant window on a wall — that image distance ≈ f. You need it to set D > 4f.
  • Fix D generously (say 4.5–5× the rough f) so the two positions are well separated and x is measurable.
  • Hunt both stations by sliding the lens; remove parallax-style hunting by approaching each sharp point from the same side.
  • Measure x between the two lens-holder marks, then f = (D²−x²)/4D.
  • Repeat for 2–3 values of D; the f values should agree within error.
