Alternating Current · Part 17

Filter Circuits

Because X_L and X_C change with frequency, a simple R-L-C network can be tuned to pass some frequencies and block others.

How impedance changes with frequency

  • R does not change with frequency — flat line
  • XL=ωL increases with frequency
  • XC=1/ωC decreases with frequency

Four basic filters

Low Pass Filter

Passes low frequencies, blocks high ones. Typically a series resistor with a shunt capacitor — X_C is large (blocking) at low f, small (bypassing) at high f.

High Pass Filter

Passes high frequencies, blocks low ones. Typically a series capacitor with a shunt resistor — X_C is small (passing) at high f, large (blocking) at low f.

Band Pass Filter

Passes only a band of frequencies around resonance — built by combining a high-pass and low-pass filter in series, or using a series LC (acceptor) stage.

Band Reject Filter

Blocks a specific band, passes everything else — built around a parallel LC (rejector) stage placed in the signal path.

Sharper filters — π and T sections

  • A single L-C stage gives a gentle roll-off; cascading stages sharpens the cutoff
  • π-filter: a series element flanked by two shunt elements (shaped like the Greek letter π)
  • T-filter: a shunt element flanked by two series elements (shaped like the letter T)
  • Both are just repeated/rearranged L-C sections built from the same series/parallel resonance ideas already covered
Filters are simply resonance and reactance-vs-frequency behaviour, applied deliberately
