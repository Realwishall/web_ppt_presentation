Alternating Current · Part 14

Sharpness of Resonance & Quality Factor

Not every resonance peak looks the same — how narrow or wide it is has its own number.

Sharp vs broad resonance

  • Two circuits can have the same resonant frequency ω0 but very different peak shapes
  • A sharper, taller peak means the circuit responds strongly only very close to ω0 — this is the better tuning circuit
  • A lower resistance R gives a sharper resonance curve

Half power points & bandwidth

  • At resonance: Z=R (minimum), I=Imax, power is maximum
  • The half power points are the two frequencies ω1, ω2 either side of ω0 where power drops to half its maximum value
  • At these points, current I=Imax/2 and impedance Z=2R
  • Bandwidth: Δω=ω2−ω1=R/L — the width of the resonance peak

Quality factor (Q)

  • Quality factor measures how sharp the resonance is relative to its frequency: Q=ω0Δω
  • Equivalent forms: Q=ω0LR=1ω0RC=1RL/C
  • Higher Q → narrower bandwidth → sharper resonance → better tuning / better ability to reject nearby frequencies
Remember: at resonance, Z is minimum & I is maximum; at the half-power points, power is half and both I, Z shift by a factor of √2
