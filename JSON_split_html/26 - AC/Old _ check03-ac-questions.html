Choke coil

A bulb needs 60 V but the supply is 100 V. How do we drop the extra voltage?

Use a resistor

Drops 40 V (60+40=100) but wastes power as heat.

Use a choke (inductor)

Voltages add as vectors: 100=602+x2⇒x=80 V, with no power loss.

Ideal choke coil = pure inductor — it shares the voltage but dissipates no power. That is why chokes (not resistors) control current in AC devices like tube-lights.

Real vs ideal choke coil

Real choke coil

Inductor + Resistor (the winding has some resistance, so a little power is lost).

Ideal choke coil

Inductor only — zero resistance, zero power loss.

AC quantities & their graphs

  • Conductance G=1/R
  • Reactance XL=ωL, XC=1/ωC
  • Susceptance B=1/X
  • Impedance Z (with the imaginary form Z=R+jX)
  • Admittance Y=1/Z (siemens, S)

Discuss

Rate of energy stored is maximum when …?

For an inductor, stored energy is 12Li2, so dUdt=Lididt — the rate of storage is greatest where the product of current and its rate of change is largest, not at the peaks.
