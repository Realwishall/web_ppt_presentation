Alternating Current · Part 11

LC Oscillations

Charge a capacitor, connect it to an inductor, and energy shuttles back and forth forever — the electrical twin of SHM.

How an LC circuit oscillates

  • A charged capacitor C is connected to an inductor L — the LC circuit
  • Capacitor discharges through L; the inductor's back-emf keeps current flowing even after the capacitor is empty, recharging it with reversed polarity
  • Energy shuttles between the electric field of C and the magnetic field of L, over and over
  • Neither an ideal capacitor nor an ideal inductor can lose energy — with no resistance, this oscillation never dies out
  • Angular frequency of oscillation: ω=1LC

The mechanical analogy

Electrical (LC)Mechanical (SHM)
Charge, qDisplacement, x
Current, i = dq/dtVelocity, v = dx/dt
Inductance, L — opposes change in iMass, m — opposes change in v
1/C — "stiffness" of the circuitSpring constant, k

L is called "electrical inertia" — exactly like mass resists a change in velocity, an inductor resists a change in current.

Worked questions

Question
A capacitor is charged to voltage V, then connected to an inductor L. Find the maximum current.
Energy conservation: 12CV2=12Limax2 ⇒ imax=VC/L
Question
Find the current in the inductor at the instant it holds 3 times the energy of the capacitor.
Total energy E=EL+EC. Given EL=3EC ⇒ EL=34E. Since E=12Limax2, i=imax3/4

LC oscillator in practice (with R)

  • A real circuit always has some resistance — energy slowly leaks out as heat
  • The amplitude decays exponentially with time (a damped oscillation)
  • But the frequency stays essentially constant — the oscillation just gets weaker, not slower
  • An LC tank circuit is used to convert DC to AC, and is called a tank circuit because it stores energy
