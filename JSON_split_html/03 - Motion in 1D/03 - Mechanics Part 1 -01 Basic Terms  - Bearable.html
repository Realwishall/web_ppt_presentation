Motion in 1D

01  ·  Basic Terms

What is î, ĵ, k̂ ?

Unit vectors: they carry direction only. A number in front carries the size.

A boy moves 4 m in the x direction4î
A boy moves 4 m in the y direction4ĵ
A boy moves 4 m in the z direction4k
A boy moves 4 m in the −x direction4(−î)  or  −4î
A boy moves 4 m in x and 3 m in y4î + 3ĵ

Basic terms

Reference frame

A frame from which observations are being made.

Reference point

A point on the reference frame from which observations are being made.

Think

The man in the train and the woman on the platform sit in different frames — and they will not agree on who is moving.

Position

Position vector

r = x î + y ĵ + z k

Magnitude   r or |r| = x² + y² + z²

Direction   r = r|r|

Example · particle at (3, 4, 5)
r = 3î + 4ĵ + 5k
|r| = 3² + 4² + 5² = 52
r = 3î + 4ĵ + 5k52

Rest and motion

Definition

If the position of a body is changing with respect to a reference point, the body is in motion w.r.t. that reference point.

Key idea

Rest and motion are relative terms.

The seated passenger is at rest w.r.t. the train, and in motion w.r.t. the platform — both statements are true at once.

Distance

Definition

Length of the actual path travelled is called distance.

Distance · Question

Question

A boy travels 4 m north and then 3 m east. Find the distance travelled.

Solution

Distance adds up along the path — direction is irrelevant.

= 4 + 3 = 7 m

Note

Displacement here would be 5 m — but that is a different quantity. Distance never cancels.

Displacement

Definition

Displacement is basically change in position.

s = rfinal − rinitial = Δr

Magnitude of displacement = shortest distance

Displacements add as vectors:

snet = s1 + s2 + s3 … + sn

Displacement · Question

Question

A body starts moving from (3, 4) and reaches (8, 8). Find the displacement.

Solution
s = rfinal − rinitial = Δr
s = (8î + 8ĵ) − (3î + 4ĵ)
s = 5î + 4ĵ
Note

The path taken between the two points does not matter at all — only where you started and where you finished.

Displacement · Question

Question

Find the displacement if the body moves on the path shown.

Solution

Net movement: 2L along x, L along y.

= 2L î + L ĵ

| s | = (2L)² + L² = L5

Rolling wheel · Question

Question

Find the displacement of the indicated point when the wheel turns by half a rotation while rolling forward.

Solution

Centre travels half the circumference = πR. The point climbs from the ground to the top = 2R.

s = πR î + 2R ĵ

| s | = π²R² + 4R² = Rπ² + 4

Rolling wheel · Question

Question

Same wheel, same half rotation — but now the marked point starts at the front of the wheel (level with the centre).

Solution

The centre still advances πR, but the point moves from +R to −R relative to the centre — it goes backwards by 2R.

s = πR − 2R

Rolling wheel · Question

Question

And once more — the marked point now starts at the back of the wheel, level with the centre.

Solution

The point moves from −R to +R relative to the centre — this time the two contributions add.

s = πR + 2R

Pattern

Same wheel, same rotation, three different answers — displacement depends on which point you follow.

Fixing directions

Before solving any direction question, pin the compass to the unit vectors.

Displacement · Question

Question

A particle first goes 3 m east, then 4 m west. Find the displacement.

Solution
= 3î − 4î
= − î  (1 m, towards west)
Compare

Distance here is 7 m, displacement is only 1 m. Opposite directions cancel in one, never in the other.

Displacement · Question

Question

A particle first goes 3 m east, then 4 m north. Find the displacement.

Solution
= 3î + 4ĵ
| s | = 3² + 4² = 5 m

Displacement · Question

Question

A particle goes 3 m east, then 4 m north, then 5 m up. Find the displacement.

Solution
= 3î + 4ĵ + 5k
= 3² + 4² + 5²
= 52 m
Question

A particle goes from 3î + 4ĵ to 5î + 7ĵ. Find the displacement.

Solution
s = rfinal − rinitial
s = (5î + 7ĵ) − (3î + 4ĵ)
s = 2î + 3ĵ

Displacement · Question tricky

Question

A particle goes 3 m east, then 4 m north, then 52 m south-west. Find the displacement.

Key step

South-west means equal parts south and west. Resolve it first:

52 at 45°  →  5(−î) + 5(−ĵ)

Solution
= 3î + 4ĵ − 5î − 5ĵ
= −2î − ĵ

Velocity

Instantaneous velocity

Rate of change of position is called velocity.

v = drdt

= dxdt î + dydt ĵ + dzdt k

Reading it

Differentiate each coordinate separately. The unit vectors just come along for the ride.

x = 3t²,  find vv = 6t
x = 3t², y = 2t,  find v6tî + 2ĵ
r = 3t²î + 2tĵ,  find v6tî + 2ĵ
Same question, twice

The last two rows are identical — one written coordinate by coordinate, one written as a single vector.

Speed

Definition

Speed = | Velocity |

Speed is just the magnitude of velocity — a scalar, and never negative.

Question

x = 3t²,  y = 2t  — find the speed.

Solution
v = 6t î + 2ĵ
speed = (6t)² + 4
Careful

Speed is not the magnitude of the average velocity, and |v| is not the same as d|r|dt. Take the derivative first, then the magnitude.

Constant velocity formulae

Linear

v = dt

d = v t     t = dv

Angular

ω = θt

θ = ω t     t = θω

Warning

These hold only while the velocity is constant. The moment the speed changes, you must go to the average-velocity definition instead.

Mixed halves · Question tricky

Question

A body travels at 90 m/s for half the distance. In the remaining half distance it travels at 20 m/s for half the time and at 40 m/s for the other half time. Find the average speed for the whole path.

Step 1 · second half (equal times)

vavg = 20 + 402 = 30 m/s

Step 2 · full path (equal distances)
vavg = 2 × 90 × 3090 + 30
= 45 m/s
Method

Collapse the inner pair into a single speed first, then treat the two halves as one equal-distance problem.

Mixed halves · Question tricky

Question

A body travels at 80 m/s for half the time. In the remaining half time it travels at 30 m/s for half that distance and at 60 m/s for the other half distance. Find the average speed.

Step 1 · second half (equal distances)

vavg = 2 × 60 × 3060 + 30 = 40 m/s

Step 2 · full path (equal times)
vavg = 80 + 402
= 60 m/s
Mirror image

Exactly the previous question with "time" and "distance" swapped — so the two formulae swap places too.

Acceleration

Definition

Rate of change of velocity.

a = dvdt

Question

if v = 3t², find a

Solution

a = dvdt = d(3t²)dt = 6t

Question

if v = 2t² î + 3t³ ĵ, find a

Solution

a = 4t î + 9t² ĵ

Question

if r = 3t⁴ î, find a

Solution
v = 3 × 4t³ î = 12t³ î
a = 12 × 3t² î = 36t² î

When velocity depends on position

The chain-rule trick

a = dvdt × dxdx

a = v dvdx

Use this whenever v is given as a function of x instead of t.

Question

if v = 3x², find a

Solution · route 1
a = dvdt = 6x dxdt
and dxdt is just the velocity
= 6x × 3x² = 18x³
Solution · route 2

a = v dvdx = 3x² × 6x = 18x³

Retardation vs negative acceleration

Retardation

If speed is decreasing, it is called retardation.

Negative acceleration

Just acceleration pointing in the negative direction. That is a statement about direction, not about slowing down.

a = −3,   v = +4

Acceleration is negative and the speed is decreasing too.

⇒ retardation ✓

a = −3,   v = −4

Acceleration is negative, but the speed is increasing.

⇒ no retardation ✗

Rule

Same signs ⇒ speeding up.   Opposite signs ⇒ slowing down. The sign of a alone tells you nothing.

Definition

aavg = change in velocitytime

Remember

"Change in velocity" is a vector subtraction — vfinal − vinitial, not a subtraction of speeds.

Terminal velocity

Definition

The final velocity reached when the acceleration becomes zero is called terminal velocity.

Why it exists

Drag grows with speed. When it exactly balances the driving force, a = 0 and the speed stops changing.

Question

If a = 12 − 2v, find the terminal velocity.

Solution
⇒ put a = 0
⇒ 0 = 12 − 2v
⇒ 2v = 12
⇒ v = 6 m/s

Reaction time

Definition

The time taken by the body to react is called reaction time.

Why it matters

For this amount of time the system cannot perform any change in the situation — the driver has not yet touched the brake.

Drop-the-ruler experiment: the distance it falls before you catch it converts straight into your reaction time.

Sign convention

Rule

One direction is taken as positive; its opposite then becomes negative.

Discipline

Choose the positive direction once, write it on the board, and keep it for the whole question. Most sign mistakes are really mid-question changes of mind.

The thread running through all six

Every one of these comes back to a single fact: distance accumulates, displacement cancels. Once a body turns around, the two part ways permanently.

One more to think about

Question

Is it possible that the velocity is zero but the acceleration is not zero?

Answer

Yes.

Throw a ball straight up. At the very top its velocity is exactly zero for an instant — but gravity is still pulling it down at 9.8 m/s² the whole time.

Velocity tells you where the body is going now. Acceleration tells you how that is about to change. They are independent.
