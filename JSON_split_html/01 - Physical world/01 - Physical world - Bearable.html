Conservation laws

Mass is conserved. True or false?
False, mass can be converted into energy — E = mc2
Kinetic energy is conserved. True or false?
No, type of energy is conserved
Mechanical energy is conserved. True or false?
No, type of energy is conserved
Volume is conserved. True or false?
Volume can be changed

The only conserved quantity are

  • Charge
  • Total energy
  • Linear momentum
  • Angular momentum

Extra information

  • Strong nuclear force is also known as quark–quark interaction
  • Gluons are the mediating particle for quark–quark interaction
  • Gravitational force acts on all particles, even light — it can even bend space and time
  • Friction force, tension, normal reaction, van der Waal forces are examples of electromagnetic force
  • Strong nuclear force is experienced by hadrons, not by leptons (for example, electron)

Extra information (contd.)

  • Strong nuclear force is the reason for stable and finite size of nuclei
  • Weak nuclear force plays a part in radioactive decay (can change particle)
  • Weak nuclear and electromagnetic together are known as electroweak
  • Mesons and baryons are part of hadrons
