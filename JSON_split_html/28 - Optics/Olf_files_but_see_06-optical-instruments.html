Two lenses in series

Compound microscope

The objective magnifies linearly, the eyepiece magnifies angularly — so the two just multiply. L is the tube length: objective to eyepiece.

Proof

Two magnifications, multiplied

The idea
The objective does the first magnification linearly; the eyepiece then treats that real image as its own object and magnifies it angularly, exactly like a simple microscope.
01
Objective forms a real, inverted image: mo= vouo
02
That image is the eyepiece's object, and the eyepiece is a magnifier: me= Dfe or 1+ Dfe
03
They act in series, so the magnifications multiply: m=mo× me
04
The tube must span object image to eyepiece, giving the length L below

Telescope — astronomical / celestial

The objective always drops a real inverted image on its own focal plane. The eyepiece then works as a plain magnifier for it, sitting ue away — so the final image can be put wherever you focus.

The minus sign
m is negative because the final image is inverted. For stars nobody minds — which is exactly why this design is called astronomical.
How to build a strong telescope
Large fo, small fe. That also makes the tube long — high magnification and a compact instrument pull against each other.
