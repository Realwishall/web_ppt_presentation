HUMAN EYE & ACCOMMODATION

Anatomy & Parameters

• Crystalline Lens: Flexible biconvex lens ($f \approx 2.27\text{ cm}$ to $2.50\text{ cm}$).

• Retina: Fixed image distance screen ($v = 2.5\text{ cm}$).

• Visual Angle ($\theta$): Angle subtended by object at eye ($\theta = \frac{h}{D}$).

Far Point (Relaxed Eye)

• Distance: $D_{\text{far}} = \infty$ (Infinite for normal eye).

• Ciliary Muscles: Fully relaxed (Flattest lens curvature).

• Max Focal Length: $f_{\text{max}} = 2.50\text{ cm}$.

$P_{\text{min}} = +40\text{ D} \quad (\text{Min Magnification})$
Near Point (Strained Eye)

• Distance: $d = 25\text{ cm}$ (Least Distance of Distinct Vision $D$).

• Ciliary Muscles: Fully contracted (Bulging lens curvature).

• Min Focal Length: $f_{\text{min}} = \frac{25}{11} \approx 2.27\text{ cm}$.

$P_{\text{max}} = +44\text{ D} \quad (\text{Max Magnification})$

MYOPIA (SHORT-SIGHTEDNESS): FAR POINT SHIFT

Myopic Ray Paths (Numerical Trace)
Physics Derivation & Correction

Defect Cause: Eyeball too long or eye lens curvature too sharp ($P_{\text{eye}}$ too high).

Far Point Condition: Far point reduces from $\infty$ to finite distance $x$ (e.g., $x = 250\text{ cm}$).

Derivation (Proof before Formula):

1. We want a lens that forms a virtual image of $u = -\infty$ at $v = -x$.

2. Thin Lens Equation: $$ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} $$

3. Substituting values: $$ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-x} - \frac{1}{-\infty} = -\frac{1}{x} $$

Corrective Focal Length: $f = -x$
Power: $P = -\frac{1}{x \text{ (in m)}} \quad (\text{Concave / Diverging Lens})$

HYPERMETROPIA: NEAR POINT SHIFT

Hypermetropic Ray Paths
Physics Derivation & Formula

Defect Cause: Eyeball too short or eye lens focal length too long ($P_{\text{eye}}$ too weak).

Near Point Condition: Near point shifts from $d = 25\text{ cm}$ to a larger distance $D'$ (e.g., $D' = 50\text{ cm}$).

Derivation (Proof before Formula):

1. Object placed at normal near point $u = -d = -25\text{ cm}$.

2. Spectacle lens must form virtual image at defective near point $v = -D' = -50\text{ cm}$.

3. Apply Thin Lens Equation: $$ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-D'} - \frac{1}{-d} = \frac{1}{d} - \frac{1}{D'} = \frac{D' - d}{d \cdot D'} $$

Focal Length: $f = \frac{d \cdot D'}{D' - d} > 0$
Power: $P = \frac{D' - d}{d \cdot D'} \quad (\text{Convex / Converging Lens})$

HYPERMETROPIA: CORRECTION WITH CONVEX LENS

Corrected Optics (Numerical: d=25cm, D'=50cm, f=+50cm)
Spectacle Correction Mechanism

• Action: The convex spectacle lens converges the strongly diverging rays coming from object at $N(25\text{ cm})$.

• Virtual Image Formation: The rays appear to originate from the eye's actual near point $N'(50\text{ cm})$.

• Eye Focusing: The eye lens easily focuses these less-divergent rays sharply onto the retina.

Worked Example ($d=25\text{cm}, D'=50\text{cm}$):
$$ f = \frac{25 \times 50}{50 - 25} = +50\text{ cm} = +0.5\text{ m} $$ $$ P = +\frac{1}{0.5\text{ m}} = +2.0\text{ D} $$

Slide 05 // Complete Correction

MYOPIA: CORRECTION WITH CONCAVE LENS

Corrected Optics (Numerical: x=250cm, f=-250cm, P=-0.4D)
Spectacle Correction Mechanism

• Action: Concave spectacle lens diverges parallel rays coming from distant objects ($u = -\infty$).

• Virtual Image: Divergent rays appear to originate from the myopic eye's far point $F(x)$.

• Sharp Focus: Since the eye can focus rays coming from $F$, the final image forms precisely on the retina.

Worked Numerical ($x = 250\text{ cm} = 2.5\text{ m}$):
$$ f = -x = -2.5\text{ m} $$ $$ P = \frac{1}{-2.5\text{ m}} = -0.4\text{ D} $$

JEE Key Exam Takeaways & Lens Separations

• Lens Distance Correction: If spectacle lens is placed at distance $d_g$ from the eye, effective distance becomes $(x - d_g)$ for Myopia and $(D' - d_g)$ for Hypermetropia.

• Presbyopia: Aging defect where near point increases due to weak ciliary muscles; corrected using Bifocal Lenses (upper concave, lower convex).
