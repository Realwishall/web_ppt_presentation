Radioactivity

The property of matter where an unstable atom spontaneously releases energy and particles (radiation) to become more stable.

The radioactive material is the substance itself — solid, liquid, or gas — that contains these unstable atoms and emits the radiation.

Energy released from one atom

A single fission frees a large burst of energy — one neutron triggers the next, and the reaction cascades.

Fission VS Fusion

Nuclear Fission

  • A heavy nucleus splits into lighter ones
  • Happens for nuclei above A = 56
  • Releases neutrons → chain reaction
  • Used in current nuclear reactors

Nuclear Fusion

  • Light nuclei combine into a heavier one
  • Happens for nuclei below A = 56
  • Releases more energy per nucleon
  • Needs very high temperature (powers the Sun)

Both climb toward the Fe-56 peak → both release energy.

Inside a fission reactor

Fission emits more than one neutron, and these are fast. To run safely we must control their number and speed.

Control rods

Rods of Cadmium absorb the extra neutrons — keeping the reaction from running away.

Moderator

Fast neutrons collide with a moderator to slow down (fast → slow). Moderators are light or heavy mass materials (e.g. water, graphite, heavy water).

Control rods manage how many neutrons; moderators manage how fast
