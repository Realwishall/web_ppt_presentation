Fusing light nuclei into helium releases

26.7 MeV

This is the reaction that powers the Sun and stars.

Why fusion is hard

  • Nuclei must overcome the Coulomb barrier — their positive charges repel strongly.
  • That needs enormous energy, supplied by very high temperature.
  • So far no device achieves a controlled, net-positive fusion reaction — including tokamak and ICF (inertial confinement).
Getting more energy out than we put in is the open challenge of fusion power
