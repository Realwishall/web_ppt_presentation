EM waves · how a field turns into a wave
1 · the source A charge carries its own field: arrows point away from it, shrinking as 1/r². Move it, and everything moves with it.
2 · the delay Nothing knows instantly. The news travels at c, so far arrows still aim at where the charge was — the break between old and new is a kink.
3 · the wave Shake the charge and the kinks never stop. The transverse part is E ∝ a⊥/c²r, and it drags a magnetic field with it: B = E/c, in phase, E ⊥ B ⊥ c.
4 · geometry How fast it fades is set by the shape of the source: point 1/r, line 1/√r, plane constant. Energy spreads over a sphere, a cylinder, or nothing at all.

1 · Static Coulomb field

Arrows point radially away from a positive charge; length ∝ 1/r². Move the charge and the whole pattern moves with it — instantly, which is the wrong physics.

2 · Retarded potentials

The field at distance r is set by where the charge was a time r/c ago. After a sudden move, arrows inside the sphere of radius ct point at the new position, arrows outside still point at the old one, and the two are joined by a travelling kink.

3 · Dipole radiation

An oscillating charge sends out kinks without stopping. The radiated field is E = −q a⊥ / 4πε₀c²r — transverse, falling as 1/r, zero along the axis of the oscillation. Its magnetic partner is B = E/c, perpendicular to both E and the direction of travel, and exactly in phase with it.

4 · Amplitude vs source geometry

Point source: energy spreads over a sphere 4πr², so I ∝ 1/r² and E ∝ 1/r.

Line source: energy spreads over a cylinder 2πrL, so I ∝ 1/r and E ∝ 1/√r.

Plane source: the area never grows, so I and E stay constant — an ideal plane wave.
