Displacement Current

The missing piece in Ampère's law — a "current" with no flowing charge.

Displacement current

Let us consider an Ampère loop around a plate of a capacitor.

∮ B→· dl→ = μoIin

Now same boundary but a different surface — bulged through the capacitor gap, where no charge crosses:

∮ B→· dl→ = 0
?
Same boundary, two surfaces, two answers — something is missing…
  • So there is a current…
  • whose cause is not flowing charge,
  • whose cause is a changing electric flux —
  • and this current is called Displacement current.

Current

Conduction current

Due to flow of charge

Ic= dqdt

Displacement current

Due to changing electric flux

Id= εo dφedt
Both produce the same effects — next: three properties
1

In the circuit  Ic=Id

Charging capacitor — the conduction current in the wire equals the displacement current in the gap.

Id=Ic= dqdt
= d(CV)dt
= ddt ( εoAl ×El )
=εo d(EA)dt
=εo dφedt =Id  ✓

It has all properties of a normal current

The displacement current in the gap produces a magnetic field, continues the circuit, and obeys Ampère's law — exactly like a conduction current.

Growing E→ between the plates — the "zone" of displacement current

3

Current in a zone is ∝ Area

As   Id= εo dφedt =εo d(EA)dt ⇒Id∝A
Full zone (area A) → full Id
Smaller zone (area A′ < A) → smaller current, I ∝ A
Enclose part of the gap → only that fraction of the current is enclosed
