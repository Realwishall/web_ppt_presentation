EM Waves · Part 5

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

From kilometre-long radio waves to gamma rays smaller than a nucleus — one family, one speed.

The spectrum

All travel at c=3×108 m/s in vacuum — only λ and f change: f=cλ

Note: higher frequency → higher photon energy → more penetrating / more dangerous

Radio waves & Microwaves

Radio waves

λ > 0.1 m
Production

Accelerated motion of charges in conducting wires (aerials).

Uses

Radio and television communication. AM band: 530 kHz – 1710 kHz; FM band: 88 – 108 MHz; cellular phones use radio waves in the ultra-high-frequency (UHF) band.

Microwaves

0.1 m – 1 mm  (GHz range)
Production

Special vacuum tubes — klystrons, magnetrons and Gunn diodes.

Uses

Radar systems for aircraft navigation and speed guns. Microwave ovens: frequency matched to the resonant frequency of water molecules, so food with water content heats up.

Infrared & Visible light

Infrared waves

1 mm – 700 nm
Production

Hot bodies and molecules (vibration of atoms and molecules). Often called heat waves.

Uses / role

Maintains Earth's warmth via the greenhouse effect — CO₂, NH₃ and water vapour absorb infrared. Infrared lamps for physical therapy, TV remote controls, Earth satellites, night-vision devices.

Visible rays

700 nm – 400 nm  (≈ 4×10¹⁴ – 7×10¹⁴ Hz)
Production

Electrons in atoms emitting light when they jump from higher to lower energy levels.

Uses / role

The part of the spectrum the human eye detects — our window to the world. Different animals sense different ranges (snakes detect infrared; many insects see ultraviolet).

Ultraviolet, X-rays & Gamma rays

Ultraviolet

400 nm – 1 nm

From the Sun, welding arcs and special lamps. Causes tanning; harmful in large doses — absorbed by the ozone layer. Used in LASIK eye surgery and germicidal (water-purifying) UV lamps.

X-rays

1 nm – 10⁻³ nm

Produced by bombarding a metal target with high-energy electrons (X-ray tube). Medical diagnostics and cancer treatment. Damages living tissue — avoid unnecessary exposure.

Gamma rays

< 10⁻³ nm  (10⁻¹⁰ – 10⁻¹⁴ m)

Emitted in nuclear reactions and radioactive decay of nuclei. Highest frequency, most penetrating. Used in medicine to destroy cancer cells.
