Four solved questions on ΦB = ∫ B·dA — from a non-uniform field over a square, to a current loop, vector fields across planes, and a tilted disc.

ΦB = ∫ B·dA = B A cos θ

When B is uniform and ⊥ to the area, just multiply. When B varies across the surface, integrate over strips.

Example · non-uniform field

Flux through a square in B = Box k

A square of side a (dimensions a × b, here a = b) lies in the x–y plane with one corner at the origin. The field points along z but grows with x: B = Box. Take a thin strip of width dx at distance x.

dΦ = B·dA = (Box)(b dx)
Φb = ∫0a Box·b dx = Bob x22 |0a
Φb = Bo b a22

Key idea: because B changes across the plate, flux is an integral of B·b·dx over strips, giving Φ = Bob a²/2.

Question 1
A large loop of radius R carries current i. Find the magnetic flux through the area πa² of a small coaxial circle at its centre. (a << R)
  • Field at the centre of the big loop: B = μ0i / 2R.
  • Since a << R, B is essentially uniform over the small circle.
  • Flux = B × (area of small circle) = B × πa².
Φ = μ0i2R × πa²
Φ = μ0 π a² i2R
Question 2
A uniform field B = B1i + B2j − B3k fills space. Find the flux through an area S lying in the (i) x–y, (ii) y–z, (iii) z–x plane.
  • Flux uses the area's normal: Φ = B·S = (component of B along the normal) × S.
  • x–y plane → normal along k → picks the −B3 term.
  • y–z plane → normal along i → picks B1.
  • z–x plane → normal along j → picks B2.
(i)  Φ1 = −B3S
(ii)  Φ2 = B1S
(iii)  Φ3 = B2S

The sign is negative in (i) because the field's z-component points along −k.

Takeaway: a plane "sees" only the field component perpendicular to it — the one along its normal.

Question 3 · CBSE AIPMT 2008
A disc of radius 0.2 m sits in a uniform field B = 1/π Wb/m² with its axis at 60° to B. The flux linked with the disc is:
a0.02 Wb
b0.06 Wb
c0.08 Wb
d0.01 Wb

Tap a letter to answer.

Φ = B A cos θ = 1π × π(0.2)² × cos60° = 0.02 Wb

The axis is the normal, so θ = 60°. A = π(0.2)² cancels the 1/π, leaving 0.04 × ½ = 0.02 Wb → option (a).

Recap

Four ways flux shows up

  • Non-uniform field: integrate over strips — Φ = Boba²/2 for B = Box k.
  • Field of a current loop: uniform over a tiny coaxial circle — Φ = μ0πa²i / 2R.
  • Vector field, chosen plane: only the component along the plane's normal counts — −B3S, B1S, B2S.
  • Tilted disc: the cos θ factor — Φ = BA cos θ = 0.02 Wb.

One rule underneath them all: Φ = ∫ B·dA. Uniform ⊥ field → multiply; varying field → integrate; tilted → cos θ.
