Wattless current

Resolve current about the voltage:

  • Icosθ — in phase, does the work.
  • Isinθ — perpendicular, does no work.

The wattless current is Isinθ.

LC circuit

Neither the capacitor nor the inductor can lose energy.

So energy just oscillates back and forth between the electric field of C and the magnetic field of L.

Energy in the LC oscillation

Total energy is conserved as it shuttles between C and L:

12CV2=12LI2=12Li2+12Cv2
  • Used to convert DC to AC.
  • Called a tank circuit as it stores energy.

Ways to solve numericals

  • First check the supply: AC or DC? Cells and batteries are DC.
  • Frequency of DC is zero → XL=ωL=0 (inductor is just a wire).
  • XC=1ωC=∞ — a capacitor blocks DC (perfect insulator).
  • All instruments read RMS; a DC instrument will not work on AC.

More solving tips

  • If a value is simply given, it is the RMS value.
  • But in V=100sinωt, the "100" is the peak value V0.
  • These phasor formulae work for amplitude / RMS, not instantaneous values (for those, just use KCL & KVL).
  • If frequency changes, remember to update XL and XC.
  • Remember: Hz is the unit of f, not ω (which is rad/s).

In an RLC series circuit

  • Current is the same everywhere.
  • Voltage as vectors: VL & VC opposite, VR perpendicular; resultant = supply voltage.
  • Reactance as vectors: XL & XC opposite, R perpendicular; resultant = Z.
  • At resonance current is maximum — the acceptor circuit.
  • If XL>XC: voltage is leading.

In an RLC parallel circuit (not in course)

  • Voltage is the same across all branches.
  • Current as vectors: IL & IC opposite, IR perpendicular; resultant = supply current.
  • Use 1/R as vectors: 1/XL & 1/XC opposite; resultant = 1/Z.
  • At resonance current is minimum — the rejector circuit.
  • If XL>XC: current is leading.
