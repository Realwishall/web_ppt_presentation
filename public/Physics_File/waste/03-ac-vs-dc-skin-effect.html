Alternating Current · Part 3

AC vs DC & the Skin Effect

A current that never changes direction, and one that reverses fifty times a second — they don't even travel through a wire the same way.

Direct vs Alternating Current

Direct Current (DC)

A DC current does not change its direction with time.

Alternating Current (AC)

Direction of current changes alternately with time (mains supply ≈ 50 Hz in India).

DC → battery / cell  ·  AC → mains supply from the power plant

Skin effect

  • The tendency of AC to flow only near the surface of a conductor is called the Skin Effect
  • Cause: the changing current sets up a changing flux that links the core more strongly than the surface, inducing a back-emf that opposes current at the centre more than at the edge
  • Result: most of the current is squeezed into a thin outer layer — the interior carries almost none at high frequency

Working around the skin effect

  • Since the core carries almost no current, AC transmission uses hollow conductors — same surface, less material
  • To keep the total conducting surface area unchanged while staying flexible, a bundle of thin wires (stranded cable) is used instead of one thick solid wire
  • This is purely an AC phenomenon — a steady DC current distributes itself uniformly across the entire cross-section
Effective resistance of a wire is therefore higher for AC than for DC at the same wire size
