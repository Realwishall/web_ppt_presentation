Alternating Current · Part 10

Power in an AC Circuit

When voltage and current are out of phase, not all the "supplied" power actually does work.

Average power & power factor

  • Instantaneous power p=vi; averaging over a full cycle for v=V0sinωt, i=i0sin(ωt−φ) gives:
  • Pavg=VrmsIrmscosφ
  • cosφ is called the power factor — the fraction of "apparent" supply that becomes real, usable power
  • Apparent power = VrmsIrms (units: VA) — what the supply seems to deliver, before accounting for phase

Power is lost only in the resistor

  • For a pure resistor: φ=0, cosφ=1 → full power dissipated
  • For a pure inductor or capacitor: φ=90°, cosφ=0 → average power = 0, even though current flows!
  • This current that flows but delivers zero net power is called wattless current — energy is only ever "borrowed" and returned each cycle, never dissipated
  • In any real R-L-C combination, real power loss happens only across the resistor — never across an ideal L or C
Neither an ideal capacitor nor an ideal inductor can permanently lose energy
