Alternating Current · Part 4

Measuring AC vs DC

Not every meter can "see" a current that reverses fifty times a second.

Moving-coil / Tangent Galvanometer

  • These work normally with DC — deflection is proportional to steady current
  • With AC of a very low frequency (1–2 Hz) the needle can physically follow the current and will oscillate
  • With mains AC (50 Hz) the coil's inertia can't respond that fast — net deflection is driven by the average torque, which is proportional to iavg over a cycle = 0. The needle simply will not move
Reason: torque ∝ current (not current²) — so it averages to zero for a symmetric AC

Hot-wire galvanometer / ammeter

  • Works on the heating effect of current — a wire expands due to i²R heating, and the expansion deflects a pointer
  • Since i2 is never negative, this deflection never cancels — it works for both AC and DC supply
  • What it actually reads is the RMS value of the current (mean of i2, then square-rooted)
  • Because deflection ∝i2, the scale is non-uniform — markings crowd together at low current and spread out at high current
  • Any type of wire works (heating doesn't care about skin effect the way hollow/bundle conductors do)
